Angle niceness

Every point at angle x gets a number D = [\mathbb{Q}(\cos x, \sin x) : \mathbb{Q}] — the degree of the field its two coordinates generate. D = 1 when \cos x and \sin x are rational; the more irrational they are, the bigger D — so low D means a nice angle. Writing x/2\pi as a fraction k/n in lowest terms: D = \varphi(n)/2 if 4 \mid n, else \varphi(n).
Raise the slider to allow larger D; tap any point for its exact coordinates and a step-by-step derivation of its D. The picture is the complete set of angles with D \le 10: a proved bound (n \le 2(2D+1)^2, see AngleNicenessSpec.lean) caps the possible denominators, so nothing is missing — and the set is exactly symmetric under reflection across the axes and the 45^{\circ} diagonals.
All values and derivations are computed and checked in Lean (AngleNiceness.lean); the page only draws them.
